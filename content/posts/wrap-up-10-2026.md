### Notable reads

[Some final words on Foursquare.](https://teendrama.substack.com/p/some-final-words-on-foursquare?r=2es81&utm_medium=ios&triedRedirect=true) 

Founder shares their POV and rant about the unfortunate ending of Foursquare, who was acquired by competitor. The saddest part is the acquisition renders all equities zero value, meaning no equity holders (including (ex)employees) get any upside in this exit. Not sure how common this is for an acquisition but the upside in equity is always (maybe not only) the major incentive for employees and investors. 

I don’t intend to write another business analysis on startups, but I’m a power user of Foursquare - my travel section below is entirely powered by Foursquare. I still think a simple check-in is still the best way to document one’s journeys while not having them dominate the trips themselves. 

To be fair the app itself is practically in the maintenance mode since the company pivoted to enterprise, but now its ending seems even closer. Unfortunately the app is not very trivial to replicate, because while the user facing functionality is simple, maintaining high quality places data for users to check in is a quite complex problem that involves both machine learning and community moderation. It is very sad that Foursquare eventually was not able to find a viable business plan to support it. Foursquare starts out as a pure social network app for consumers (a lot more like Instagram, Snapchat, etc), but its business plan is to sell data and technology to enterprise. 

When Foursquare enters the market it was a hit initially. On the data side there’s strong demand (Uber, Apple, Samsung all become customer) and limited supply (Google might be the only one and maybe Facebook, none really invests in this area and arguably is a competitor). The technology (SDK) is also truly cutting edge and gaining tractions. But running an enterprise business that sells to companies is so different from a consumer one. A successful pivot requires many things to align, and for Foursquare some didn’t. The ex-president of Foursquare has a good write-up on some of it. There are many successful pivots (Dropbox, 1Password), but Foursquare is sadly not among them.

While coding activity is dropping I get some major upgrades over some of my repos. The biggest one is automating Apple Health data export, which is where my monthly workout data is from. The Apple Health iOS app support manual export but that’s so easy to forget and I have forgotten to do so many times. 

The solution is an automated shortcut that uploads the export to a server that persists the data. A shortcut is basically a workflow that one defines in the iOS system app Shortcuts that can do stuff for the user. For example, one can set up a shortcut to save the current Safari web page into a pdf. The app also offers simple automation like fire the shortcut every day at 8AM etc. Each app, depends on the developer, offers some triggers and functionality. The Apple Health app itself does not have any, but there is an app called Actions does that - it’s like a power pack for Shortcuts, expanding more functionalities of the system apps, including Apple Health. With that app, I was able to setup an automatic export of Apple Heath data as an automation in Shortcuts app. It also takes the export (a .xml file) and uploads to a server I host on Cloudflare, so that I don’t have to manually copy the file, which would’ve defeated the whole automation purpose.

Thanks to the shortcut, now all my monthly data sources are automated except only one left: the weightlifting data that is sourced from the iOS app Strong.
