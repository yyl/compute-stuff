That’s according to Apple’s own [Apple Platform Security](https://help.apple.com/pdf/security/en_GB/apple-platform-security-guide-b.pdf) whitepaper, updated just last month, which states: > The probability that a random person in the population could unlock a user’s iPad, iPhone, or Apple Vision Pro is less than 1 in 1 million with Optic ID or Face ID — including when Face ID with a mask is turned on. For a user’s iPad, iPhone, Mac models with Touch ID, and those paired with a Magic Keyboard with Touch ID, it’s less than 1 in 50,000. This probability increases with multiple enrolled fingerprints (up to 1 in 10,000 with five fingerprints) or appearances for Face ID (up to 1 in 500,000 with two appearances).

#### Benedict's Newsletter: No. 658 (articles)

> Musk musks The Information does a good job of pulling together all of Elon Musk’s recent moves into making his own gas turbines to power data centres. As a bunch of people have pointed out (see the Bloomberg report I linked last week), there are more chips being bought and data centres being built in the US than there is grid power to light them all up, with supply backlogged for years, and if you want to build your own on-site generation then gas turbines are the best option, except that there are only a handful of suppliers and they’re backlogged for years as well. Further along the supply chain, the turbine blades themselves are the critical components and those again have a handful of backlogged suppliers. So, in parallel to SpaceX’s plan to create a trillion dollar chip foundry, Musk now plans to start making his own turbine blades. The whole thing looks more and more like Henry Ford (with Twitter standing in for the Dearborn Independent). I know enough about cellular to know that SpaceX cannot compete with it directly, but I don’t know enough about gas turbines to know if this makes any sense.
