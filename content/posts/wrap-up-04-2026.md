I spent a mere 4 hours exercising - it sounds like so little after the 34 hours of podcasts! But I've picked up indoor climbing (bouldering) recently, and it's been a lot of fun. Bouldering is a form of rock climbing that focuses on short, technical movements. It is done without ropes. Gyms specifically design the shapes and layouts of the rocks on the wall so that a certain set of adjacent holds forms a route that people can climb from beginning to end. As a visual clue, the holds on the same route usually share the same color. 

There are a few things that make bouldering quite unique:

1. It combines cardio, strength training, brain exercise, and entertainment:
	* Cardio: The entire route usually takes 5–10 moves that require sustained stamina to power through.
	* Strength: Obviously, it activates many different kinds of muscles.
	* Brain exercise: Each route only marks the beginning and end. There can sometimes be multiple ways to climb one route, so you need to think it through first.
	* Entertainment: It is a lot of fun because the route-planning part is like a puzzle, while the climbing part is a physical challenge.
2. It puts you in "danger mode," but is actually very safe:
	* When climbing on the wall without any protection, especially at certain heights, you naturally sense the danger of falling and a fear of heights.
	* At the same time, all walls are surrounded by soft pads, so when people actually fall (and people *do* fall!), they rarely get hurt.
3. It's highly accessible: It's indoors and does not require special equipment or skills, making it accessible to everyone - men, women, seniors, and kids.

The danger bit stands out for me. Despite knowing how safe it is, and having personal experience with it (I've fallen so many times!), every time I'm on the wall, the sense of danger just kicks in. It helps me hyper-focus in the moment and not think about anything else - I have no capacity to do so anyway! Once I finish a climb, though, that intense focus goes away and I feel refreshed. It is a little bit like surfing, but because of its slower motion, the feeling is more prominent and deeper.
